 Here is the question I want you to keep in mind all semester: how does the cost grow when the input gets bigger?
 Look at the two ways to add one to n. The loop does n additions. The formula does a constant number of operations, no matter what n is.
 When we count cost, we count the basic operation. Students often count every line of code. Don't do that. Pick the operation in the innermost loop.
 For linear search, the worst case is when the key is not there at all, so we compare with every element.
 Now the definition of Big-O. You need two constants, c and n zero. Write them down explicitly in the exam, otherwise you lose marks.
 A common mistake: people think Big-O means the exact growth rate. It is only an upper bound. n is in O of n squared, that's true.
 Big-Omega is the lower bound. You won't be asked to prove a lower bound formally, but you should be able to use the notation.
 Finally, memorise this chain of growth rates. Log n, n, n log n, n squared, two to the n. That ordering comes back in every lecture.
